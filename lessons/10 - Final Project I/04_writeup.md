# Writing Up Your Findings

**Objective**: Write a `summary.md` that states your question, your data, your findings, and their limitations — and submit Phase 1 as a pull request.

---

Analysis that stays in a notebook is only half finished. This module produces `summary.md`, a short written account of what you found. It is a graded deliverable this week, and it does double duty: in Week 11 it becomes the script for your presentation video. Writing it well now means you are not composing under deadline later.

## What `summary.md` Contains

Keep it to about one page, with four parts:

1. **The question.** What you set out to answer, in a sentence or two.
2. **The data.** Which dataset you used, and anything a reader needs to know about it (size, time span, notable gaps).
3. **The findings.** Your three or more findings, each stated in plain language and pointing to the chart that supports it. This is the heart of the document — say *what you found*, not just what you did.
4. **The limitations.** What your analysis can't conclude, and any biases or gaps in the data. Naming limitations honestly is a mark of good analysis, and the rubric rewards it.

## Write It to Be Spoken

Because `summary.md` becomes your Week 11 presentation script, write it in clear, plain language you could read aloud. A finding written as *"Documentaries show the highest median return on budget, roughly three times that of action films, though they earn far less in absolute revenue"* is ready both to read and to say. Avoid jargon a listener couldn't follow.

## Submit Phase 1

This week's submission has two parts:

* **The Kaggle notebook** — your analysis, shared as a public link.
* **A pull request** to your project repository containing `summary.md` and your cleaned data file.

Committing the cleaned data now means Phase 2's dashboard has something to read next week.

---

## Common Pitfalls

* **Describing what you did instead of what you found.** "I grouped by genre and made a bar chart" is a method, not a finding. State the result.
* **No limitations.** Every analysis has them. Omitting them reads as overconfidence and loses rubric credit.
* **Findings not supported by the analysis.** Every claim in the summary should trace back to a chart or number in your notebook.
* **Forgetting the cleaned data.** The pull request needs both `summary.md` and the cleaned data file — Phase 2 depends on the data being there.
