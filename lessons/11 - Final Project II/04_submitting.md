# Recording and Submitting

**Objective**: Record a short presentation of your project using `summary.md` as your script, and submit the final project.

---

The last step is to present your work and submit it. You have already written most of what you will say — this module turns `summary.md` into a short video and walks through the submission.

## Record Your Presentation

Record a **3–5 minute** video that presents your project. Your `summary.md` from Week 10 is your script: it already states the question, the data, the findings, and the limitations, in language meant to be read aloud. Use it as your outline rather than composing something new.

A good presentation covers:

* **The question** — what you set out to answer, and why the dataset fits it.
* **The findings** — your key results, shown on the dashboard or your charts. Demonstrate the dashboard live: change a filter and show the numbers and charts respond.
* **The limitations** — what your analysis cannot conclude.

Speak in your own words; you do not need to script it line by line. Record by screen-sharing so viewers see the dashboard running. If your deployment had trouble, demonstrate the app locally — a working local demo is fine.

Upload the video to YouTube (unlisted) or Loom, and add the link to your repository's `README.md`.

## Submit the Final Project

Phase 2 is submitted as a **single pull request** to your project repository. Before you open it, confirm your repository contains:

* `streamlit_app.py` — the dashboard.
* `cleaned_data.csv` — the data the dashboard reads.
* `requirements.txt` — the dependencies.
* `README.md` — summary, setup steps, screenshot, deployed URL, and video link.
* `service_urls.txt` — the deployed URL.

Open the pull request and submit its link on the assignment submission form. Because the deployed URL and the video link live in the repository, the pull request is the only link you submit.

## Common Pitfalls

* **Composing the talk from scratch.** `summary.md` is already your script — use it instead of starting over.
* **Describing instead of demonstrating.** Show the dashboard running and the filters working, not just slides about it.
* **A video that is too long.** Keep it to 3–5 minutes; practice once to check the length.
* **A forgotten link.** The video link and deployed URL must be in the README before you open the pull request.
